I was on a train last week. No signal. No Wi-Fi.
And I was having a conversation with an AI.






Not a cached page. Not a pre-downloaded chatbot.
A real language model running entirely on my phone.

I put my phone in airplane mode just to test it.
Asked it to explain a concept. It responded instantly.
Asked it to help me draft something. It did.
No internet. No API. No cloud. Nothing.

That moment hit different.

We've been trained to think of AI as something
that lives on someone else's server.
Something that needs a subscription, a login,
and a stable connection to function.

But what if it didn't?

What if AI was just... there?
On your phone. Always. Like a calculator.

That's what's happening right now with small models.
And most people haven't noticed yet.

I think about this from the lens of access.
Not everyone lives in a city with fast broadband.
Not every country has reliable cloud infrastructure.
Not every user can afford a monthly AI subscription.

But almost everyone has a phone.

When AI can run on a phone without internet,
it stops being a premium product.
It becomes infrastructure.

which models to use, what phones support it,
and how to set it up on iPhone and Android.

The smartest AI won't always be the biggest.
Sometimes it'll be the one in your pocket.

Skill doesn't pay the bills. Output does.



I spent 15 years in performance engineering.
I've worked in Belgium, Netherlands, led teams at big consulting firms.
On paper, I looked like someone who had it figured out.

Then I got into Gen AI around September last year.

I did what I always do. Learn first, build later.
Watched tutorials. Read papers. Took notes nobody would ever read.
I could explain RAG pipelines, vector databases, embedding models.
At parties. To people who didn't ask.

Six months of that.

You know what I had to show for it? A very organized Notion page.

Meanwhile I kept seeing people online shipping actual things.
Ugly apps. Broken demos. Stuff that made me think "I could do better than that."

But they were out there. And I was still "preparing."

Here's the thing nobody tells you about preparing:
It feels exactly like progress. Same dopamine hit.
Except at the end, you have notes. They have users.

What finally broke the cycle for me was embarrassment, honestly.
I pushed out a rough project. Messy code. UI that looked like it was built in 2012.
Posted it anyway.

Got more response in two weeks than six months of learning ever gave me.

Some things I keep telling myself now:

. Nobody cares about your Notion page. Ship something.
. "Getting ready" is just procrastination with better lighting.
. One ugly app that exists beats ten perfect apps in your head.
. People hire track records. Not potential.

I still catch myself slipping back into tutorial mode sometimes.
Old habits. But now I notice it faster.

If you're sitting on skills and wondering why nothing's moving,
this might be the reason.

"If you don't produce, you won't thrive, no matter how skilled or talented you are."

That line hit me. Maybe it'll hit you too.

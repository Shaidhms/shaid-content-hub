The worst leaders I've worked with all had one thing in common.


They took credit when things went well. And hid when things went wrong.

15 years in consulting. Big firms. Enterprise clients. Belgium, Netherlands, India. I've seen enough leadership to fill a book nobody would buy.

Bad leader: "We delivered ahead of schedule."
(Translation: the team pulled three all-nighters while I updated the status report.)

Same leader when something breaks: "I'll need to look into who was responsible for this."

Meanwhile the best manager I ever had did something I still think about. A deployment went sideways in the Netherlands. Client was furious. This guy walked into the call, said "This was my call, I'll fix it," and took the full hit.

He didn't even work on that module.

We would've done anything for him after that. And we basically did. Next three months we shipped like it was personal.

That's the sacrifice Sinek talks about. Not the "I skipped lunch" kind.

. The leaders people actually follow are the ones who take the hit first.
. "I sacrifice for the team" usually means "I stay till 6 PM sometimes."
. If your sacrifice needs announcing, it probably isn't one.
. People don't follow titles. They follow the person who showed up when it got ugly.

"Great leaders are willing to sacrifice their own interests for the good of those who follow them." - Simon Sinek

Still looking for more of those leaders. They're rare.

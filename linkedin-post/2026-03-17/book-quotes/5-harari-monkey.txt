Your company's "future growth plan" is just bananas you'll never see.


I've sat through enough town halls to know the script.

"We're investing in your growth."
"The next two quarters are going to be exciting."
"We see you in a leadership position soon."

Translation: keep doing what you're doing, for what we're paying you now, and maybe something will happen. Eventually. Possibly.

Harari wrote something in Sapiens that I think about every performance review season:

"You could never convince a monkey to give you a banana by promising him limitless bananas after death."

The monkey is smarter than most of us here.

The monkey wants the banana now. Can see it. Can hold it. Doesn't care about your banana roadmap.

But we'll sit through a 45-minute review, hear "you're on the right track," get a 3% raise that doesn't cover inflation, and call it progress.

I did this for years. "Next year will be different." "The promotion cycle is coming." "They see my potential."

Then I did the math. 15 years of experience. Earning 11 LPA. Meanwhile people with half my experience were pulling 25+ because they didn't wait for the banana. They went and found their own tree.

. Promises are free. Paychecks are real.
. If the "growth plan" doesn't have a date and a number, it's fiction.
. Be the monkey. Want the banana now.
. Loyalty to a company that won't invest in you isn't loyalty. It's just habit.

The monkey knows better. Maybe we should listen.

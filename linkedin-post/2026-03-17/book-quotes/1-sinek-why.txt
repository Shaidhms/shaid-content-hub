Nobody cares what you build. They care why you built it.


I spent years selling "what" to clients.

"We do performance testing."
"We do AI automation."
"We build websites."

Cool. So does everyone else on Fiverr for $5.

The projects that actually landed came after I stopped pitching features and started talking like a human being.

"After 15 years in enterprise tech, I started building AI tools on the side because I think small businesses shouldn't need a tech team to use AI."

That's not a pitch. It's just... the actual reason I'm doing this.

Had a call with a potential client few months ago. Spent 20 minutes explaining my tech stack. His eyes glazed over around minute three. I could see it on the Zoom call. The man was mentally ordering dinner.

Then I accidentally said something real. Told him why I left Belgium. Why I gave up the comfort to come back and build from scratch.

He leaned forward. "Tell me more about that."

One honest sentence did more than my entire portfolio deck.

Some things I keep reminding myself:

. Your "what" is a commodity. Your "why" is yours.
. If you can't explain why you do this, don't expect clients to explain why they picked you.
. People buy conviction. Not credentials.

"People don't buy what you do; they buy why you do it." - Simon Sinek

I read that line in Start with Why and haven't stopped thinking about it since.

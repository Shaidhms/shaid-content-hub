I started 47 side projects last year. Finished 3.


The 3 that made it? I knew what "done" looked like before I wrote the first line of code.

The other 44? I just started building because the idea was exciting. No end state. No finish line. Just vibes and a new GitHub repo.

"Let me just set up the boilerplate."
"I'll figure out the scope later."
"This is just a POC."

Famous last words. Every single time.

Covey says "Begin with the end in mind" and I used to think that was obvious. Like yeah, obviously. Who starts something without knowing where it's going?

Me. I do that. Regularly.

What changed: I started writing one sentence before every project.

"This is done when ___."

"This is done when the client can upload a PDF and get a summary."
"This is done when I can screenshot it and post on LinkedIn."
"This is done when someone pays me for it."

That one sentence killed more scope creep than any project management tool I've ever paid for.

. Starting is easy. Finishing is the actual skill.
. If you can't describe "done" in one sentence, you're not ready to start.
. A finished ugly thing beats ten perfect things in your head.

"Begin with the end in mind." - Stephen Covey

Simple advice. Took me 15 years and 44 abandoned repos to actually hear it.

Nobody is born good at anything. Literally nobody.


I used to watch people build AI demos on Twitter and think "they're just naturally good at this."

Then I saw their GitHub histories.
Hundreds of commits. Broken repos. Abandoned projects from two years ago.
They weren't born good. They just started before me.

"Practice isn't the thing you do once you're good. It's the thing you do that makes you good." - Malcolm Gladwell, Outliers

I've been writing code for 15 years.
But I only started building AI products six months ago.
And the first few months? I was genuinely bad at it.

My RAG pipeline returned nonsense. My embeddings were wrong. My UI looked like a government website from 2008.

But each broken thing taught me something specific.
Not theoretical. Specific. Like "oh, chunk size actually matters" specific.

The gap between "I understand this" and "I can build this" is filled entirely by practice.
Not tutorials. Not courses. Practice.

Here's the uncomfortable part:
You have to be bad at something for a while before you're good at it.
And most people quit during the bad part.

Don't be most people.

Most people weren't part of the story. They were busy surviving.



I read this line in Sapiens and had to put the book down for a minute.

"History is something that very few people have been doing while everyone else was ploughing fields."

Think about that.

While empires rose and fell, most humans just... farmed.
They didn't choose to be on the sidelines. They didn't have the option.

But we do.

I spent years doing the equivalent of ploughing fields in tech.
Stable job. Good pay. Showing up, doing the work, going home.
Nothing wrong with that. Except I kept feeling like I was watching from the stands.

The people building AI products, posting content, launching startups?
They weren't smarter. They just decided to stop farming and start making things happen.

That's the part Harari doesn't say outright, but it's there:
History belongs to the people who chose to participate.

Not the most talented. Not the best connected.
The ones who decided their work should be seen.

You're reading this on LinkedIn. You have an internet connection and a skill set.
You're already luckier than 99% of humans who ever lived.

The question is: are you making history or ploughing fields?

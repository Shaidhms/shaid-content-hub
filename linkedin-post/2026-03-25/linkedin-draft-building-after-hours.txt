I'm trying to build a future between 9 PM and midnight.





Every day I clock out of a full-time job.
Then I open my laptop again.

Not because someone told me to.
Because I watched people with my exact skill set
get passed over for roles they should have landed.
The only difference? They stopped learning.

I spent 15 years in performance engineering.
Big companies. International projects. Steady paychecks.
It was safe. It was predictable.

Predictable doesn't keep you relevant.

AI moved faster than I expected.
Tools I was using 2 years ago feel dated now.
Frameworks I learned last quarter already have alternatives.
I'm not exaggerating. That's the actual pace.

So I started spending my evenings catching up.
Building AI products. Breaking things. Shipping real work.
All after my day job ends.

Here's the part nobody talks about:

It's not glamorous.
You skip Netflix. You skip sleep sometimes.
You say no to things you'd rather say yes to.

Your family doesn't always understand why you're
still on the laptop after a full day of work.
Your kids want your attention. Your spouse wants your time.
And you're sitting there torn between being present
and building something that could change all of your lives.

That's the hardest part honestly.
Not the late nights. The look on your kid's face
when you say "give me 30 more minutes" for the third time.

And some nights, you sit in front of your screen
with nothing left in the tank
and you still push through one more task.

That's not hustle culture.
That's a bet you placed on your family's future.

I'm not building a side project.
I'm trying to stay useful in an industry
that forgets you the moment you stop showing up.

What I've figured out doing this for the past year:

. You don't need 8 hours a day. 2 focused ones move the needle.
. Managing your energy matters more than managing your clock.
. The guilt of "not doing enough" never goes away. You just learn to work with it.
. If you haven't picked up something new this quarter, that's a problem.
. Your 9-to-5 funds the dream. Respect it, but don't let it define you.

Most people keep waiting.
For the right savings. The right idea. The right time.

I waited too. For years.
Nothing changed until I just started.

I'm not where I want to be yet.
But I know more than I did 6 months ago
and I've shipped more than I thought I could.

If you're in tech and your skills look the same
as they did last year, pay attention to that.

The window to catch up is still open.
But it won't stay open forever.
